## Did

Chose the bar, and the choosing turned into a measurement. ADR-0019 splits it
into a **reality floor** and a **per-lever adoption rule**, defines "ruled out",
and sizes the bench — but the number that decides all four came out of
`tools/power/`, which reads the records we already hold and asks what they could
ever have shown.

**Eleven of the twelve bundle contrasts this project has run were unresolvable
before the model was dispatched.** Not "came out null" — unresolvable. With `m`
discordant pairs the best-case two-sided p is `2 / 2**m`, so `m >= 6` is a wall;
nine of the twelve sit below it and two more only just clear it. The method
reproduces CLM-0017's published McNemar values (0.45 / 0.12 / 0.07) and
CLM-0012's "13 of 20 condition-insensitive" exactly, which is how I know it is
reading the records rather than inventing them.

**The Python arm is 5% responsive** — one task of twenty ever moves, thirteen
are pinned passing and six pinned failing. It reads 65–70% and looks squarely in
band. That is the size of the gap #234 opened when it corrected ADR-0017 to say
the Python root is in band by *level* and not shown to be in band by *response*,
and it means **being in band and having resolution are different properties that
a pass rate cannot distinguish**.

**The instruments are quiet; they are coarse because they are small.** Six to
eight greedy re-runs of a byte-identical configuration drift by **0 tasks** on
three of four models and 1 task on the fourth. The 30B is the whole finding in
one row: **a third of its replies differ byte for byte and not one verdict
does.** So #216's ±0.7pp greedy figure transfers to the 20-task instrument at
four model sizes, and under P3 that is transfer *shown*, not flagged — the first
of the three drift figures to get past that obligation.

**This corrects #229's own framing.** The issue expected drift to be the reality
floor. It is not: resolution binds, drift is an order of magnitude below it, and
a bench can post zero null drift while resolving nothing. So #231's
commissioning gate needs a second condition — `MDE <= b` alongside `drift < b`,
per tier, in writing — and it is the one that fails today.

**The adoption rule is four classes sorted by what adoption costs**, not one
number: reversible-zero-cost (prompt assembly, output caps, granularity),
ongoing-per-task (decomposition, attempts, breadth), weights, and cost-only
(#81). For a reversible lever **the bar is whatever the bench resolves** — which
is the owner's direction implemented, because nothing inherited stands between a
measured improvement and shipping. Weights carry two extra conditions, one of
them "survives a backend change", because #189 measured identical weights
swinging 2.6pp on backend alone.

**Three verdicts, and only NULL retires a lever.** Every bundle contrast we hold
is UNDECIDED, including the one CLM-0012 reports as a null. That does not
withdraw CLM-0012: what carries it is CLM-0017's **positive control**, and the
episode is the argument for ADR-0018 Q3 making one mandatory.

**Sizing is a function, not a number.** `psi` is a property of the (instrument,
lever) pair — a task pinned under the bundle may move under decomposition — so
#231 measures it at commissioning and #225 is sized from that, with our measured
0.05–0.40 as the prior. Recommendation: **n = 400**, resolving +5 to +8pp across
that range, sitting where the curve turns (3pp costs 920–3,113). Its price is
**authoring 400 contracts**, not rig time: a full 8-cell round runs in 3–5
rig-hours at the 3B's measured 2.4–3.1s per dispatch. "Only the widening costs
meaningful rig time" was the wrong axis.

**Prior art searched and recorded** (`docs/adoption-bar-prior-art-2026-08-10.md`,
eight queries and six fetches, provenance and expiry stated). The noise side has
converged since 2024 on discordance as the binding quantity — Miller, Kotawala
and one derived source independently. **The strongest external check: that
source states HumanEval resolves 6.9 points at α .05 / power .80 / 10%
discordance, and `tools/power` computes +6.9pp for n=164 at the same inputs,
written before the source was found.** It also shows #219's quoted ~+4.8pp MDE
was optimistic — it implies a 5% discordance rate.

On the gain side we did not find a published adoption threshold in what was
searched; reported gains cluster at 9–51pp, an order of magnitude above our
margin. Stated as searched-and-not-found with the search recorded.

`MIN_QUALITY_GAIN` keeps its value and its job; `propose.py` now says so where
the constant lives, and the pilot record carries an amendment converting its
"miss" to UNDECIDED — which is the caveat that record itself asked to have filed
"wherever `MIN_QUALITY_GAIN` is consumed".

## Left open

**A trap that cost me the right answer once and is worth knowing about.**
`overran_cap` is False on **all 12,466 measurement rows** in the repository
while `stop_reason == "truncated"` marks 374 of them. This is not a defect —
`runner.py:242` asks whether the backend returned *more* than it was allowed,
which never happened — but the two fields ship side by side in the same row and
the name invites the misreading. Filtering pool drift on `overran_cap` instead
of `stop_reason` turns 1 discordant problem into 3, which is a 3x error in a
published figure. Encoded as a comment in `report.py` and in
`tools/power/README.md`; nobody has filed it and it may only deserve a name.

**Not done, deliberately:** D6 names draws-per-task as the other way to buy
resolution and does not adopt it — it interacts with #119 and belongs to an arm,
not to the bar. And `psi` is measured here only for the bundle lever; every
other lever's discordance is unknown until its arm runs, which is why D5 is a
function rather than a number.
