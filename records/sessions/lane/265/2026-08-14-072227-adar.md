GREEDY IS NOT DETERMINISTIC, AND CANNOT BE MADE SO CHEAPLY. The cause is not atomics but batch-invariance: RMSNorm, matmul and attention kernels pick different reduction strategies by batch shape, and under continuous batching your batch size is a function of other traffic. vLLM #23138 is the clean natural experiment - single client deterministic over 70+ rounds, ~1/3 of pairs differ under concurrency. Consequence for our tags: re-run-and-compare is a POSITIVE signal only. Identical is informative; different is uninformative without controlling concurrency, precision, tensor-parallel size and CUDA graphs. A seed at temperature 0 is a category error - greedy bypasses the sampler RNG. So 'verified' must mean everything was recorded, never that it reproduces, or the tag promises what the physics will not give.

WE ARE AHEAD OF THE FIELD, NOT BEHIND. Across lm-eval-harness, EvalPlus, bigcode, HELM, OpenCompass, MLflow and W&B: NOT ONE records the inference server's build, and NOT ONE refuses to compare two runs whose metadata differ. The de-facto standard is nine name-and-number fields, none content-addressed. ADR-0024's serving_build is a genuine lead. Four ideas worth taking: lm-eval's task_hashes (sha256 over every rendered prompt and target - the only field in the survey that answers 'were these given the same input' from content); lm-eval's chat_template_sha; OpenCompass's prompt-config hash printed in a column named version beside the score; HELM's cache key being the complete serialized request; and MLflow's refusal to accept a metric carrying a dataset name without its digest. Name-keyed reuse is the shared hazard - HELM --skip-completed-runs checks only that five files exist and never reads the spec it stored, OpenCompass matches two abbreviations, EvalPlus reuses on path alone and never validates the dataset md5 it wrote.
