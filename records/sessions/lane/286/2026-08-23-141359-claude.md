# Session — lane/286 — 2026-08-23 (twenty-fourth)

## The ruling

The run contract's second post-state clause read **"no container of ours"** and
the reading beneath it was `docker ps`, which lists **running** containers only.
Two ways out were put to the owner: widen the check to `docker ps -a`, or narrow
the clause to "running" with its reason stated. **The owner chose to narrow.**

The reason, written into `docs/run-contract-2026-08-22.md` §4 as a dated
correction: a stopped container holds nothing this rule protects — no card, no
port, no process, and no claim on the next cell, which removes it before it
launches. Requiring its absence would make the contract's cheapest guarantee
turn on a state that costs nothing to leave behind.

**The original sentence is left byte-identical.** A pointer sits beside it and
the correction carries the change, which is ADR-0039's form. The one departure
from that form is the pointer itself: an ADR's superseded figure is harmless
because the *code* is operative, whereas §4's four clauses in check order are a
list an operator acts on directly, so a reader who stops at the list must not be
left acting on the superseded one.

## What landed

`50e69c3e`, then the corrections below.

- **The record takes `-a`** (`vllm.py:475`), so an exited container appears with
  its status and the `docker inspect` that follows recovers the argv it died on.
- **The gate does not** (`vllm.py:570`). `released` decides whether anything of
  ours still holds the card, and a stopped container holds none of it. **The
  record sees more than the gate acts on, and that asymmetry is the answer
  rather than a leftover.**
- **The other two `docker ps` reads keep their running-only form, and one of
  them matters.** `launched_width` reads `--max-num-seqs` off a container's own
  argv, and `docker ps -a` lists newest first — a sweep that added `-a`
  everywhere would answer with the width of the run that **failed**. That is a
  mutant in the sweep below, and it is killed.
- **A failed cell's container is removed at once, because its reason is read
  first.** `_launch_log` (`vllm.py:1109`) reads the engine's last 40 lines on
  the failure path and carries them, scrubbed, into the refusal. Keeping the
  container until somebody looks makes every launch depend on a human having
  been there, and a campaign runs unattended for hours.
- **The pip rig lost the reason the same way and nobody had noticed.** The
  refusal named two places to look; the container rig destroys its one with
  `docker rm -f`, and the pip rig redirects `> /tmp/vllm-serving.log`, which
  **truncates** the previous cell's rather than appending to it. Both launchers
  are read now.

Seven checks in `tests/test_serving.py`; seven mutants, one edit each, **all
seven killed**. `make check`: 1,945 passed, 14 xfailed. `make lint` clean.

## Kept

- **A fixture is only a check if the fake host discriminates.** The canary here
  puts the pre-fix question to the same host and shows the answer differs;
  asserting that `readings` contains the string `-a` would have confirmed the
  thermometer was installed and nothing more.
- **A claim about scrubbing was overstated and corrected before it shipped.** A
  first comment said the tail was scrubbed because "the argv is where a
  `-e HF_TOKEN=` lives". It is not: `contract.scrub` catches credential URLs,
  home-directory prefixes and published token shapes, and an arbitrary
  `KEY=value` an operator invented is redacted by nothing in this tree. The
  comment, the contract and the check all now say the weaker true thing.
