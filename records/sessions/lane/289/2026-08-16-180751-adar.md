**Answered the question that decided the shape of the work.** Subset reproducibility bounds are a **recomputation, never a re-run**. A bound is a function of paired verdicts over a set of cells; any subset of an already-paired set is itself already paired, so a new dispatch cannot resolve it better — it can only produce different verdicts, which is a different null. `null.compare()` already returns `shared` and `flips` as task-id lists rather than counts, so the filter is free. Demonstrated: 12 bounds out of the 4 existing nulls, reproducing ADR-0027's 10.15pp exactly. **So ADR-0027 D9 costs zero rig time** — `cells` multiplies declared bounds, not runs.

The caveat is recorded with it: every `d` is 0, so every one of those 12 is `wilson_upper(0, n)` — arithmetic on subset size, not a measurement of the subset. The machinery earns its keep the first time `d > 0`, where which cells flipped decides whether a subset inherits the drift. True and nearly empty today.

**Shipped the card** as `tools/bench/rate-card.json` + `ratecard.py` + `tests/test_bench_ratecard.py`, replacing the "about 40 minutes" constant in `reproducibility.json`'s `matching` prose. Measured, that constant was 2.5x too high for the cheapest cell and 18% too low for the dearest.

Two design choices worth naming. The card **derives from the runs each declared bound names** rather than from its own list of run directories, so a bound added or retired moves the card with it and the two cannot drift. And it is keyed on `(model, tier)` rather than `model`, on the measurement: the gate is 0.20 s/task at both models on `bench-py`, identical to two decimals, while generation scales 1.9x for 4.7x the parameters.

`tier` here means the language arm, matching `run.json` and `identity.BOUND_MATCH`. Noted in the record because the word carries three senses across the tree — a worker rung in the product ladder, a language arm in the bench, and model size in #266's prose — while `arm` in `results.jsonl` is the draw type. The card uses the BOUND_MATCH sense and says so rather than adding a fourth.
