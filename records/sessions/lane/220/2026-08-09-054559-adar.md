# Session — lane/220 — 2026-08-09

## Did

Opened #219 intending to run its three-run fine-tune design, probed before
spending, and found the design could not answer its own question. The lane
turned into #220 and an ADR.

**The probe that stopped the run.** #219 plans to re-tune the 3B and evaluate on
the #197 pool. The 3B reads **0/50** on that pool — 0/20 on the early range,
0/10 on the held-out suffix, 0/20 on the Python arms — against 78.0% on
HumanEval+ and 50.6% on the breadth `d1` tier. Every reply was well-formed
(zero parse refusals, zero truncation at cap 2048) and the failures were read by
hand rather than counted: an undeclared `match` in `p001`, an off-by-one in
`p002`'s whitespace handling. Genuine wrong answers, not an instrument artifact.

The pool is a different size class of task: **median 60-line reference solution**
(p90 104, max 233), **median 18 assertions** (p90 25, max 41), plus an admission
gate that rejects what a stub can pass. HumanEval is 5–15 line single functions.
The ladder compresses against it — 14B ~90% → 33.8%, 7B → ~19%, 3B → 0.

Two supporting probes, same 30 hash-spread problems from the unswept suffix
`p282+`: **7B 1/30 (3.3%)**, **14B 5/30 (16.7%)**. The 14B's 16.7% against its
own 33.8% baseline reproduces #212's ~0.5 batch-difficulty ratio, so the suffix
is behaving as expected rather than anomalously — it is just uniformly half as
passable, and half of the 3B's zero is still zero.

**The conclusion is not "the 3B is weak".** A model at 78% on HumanEval+ and 0%
on 60-line, 18-assert problems has a whole-problem ceiling below the pool's unit
of work. Closing that gap is what decomposition, the pipeline and the gates are
for. The pool measures capability at a granularity mcgyvr is designed never to
hand a small worker — it separates 7B from 14B cleanly, which makes it a
**ceiling instrument**, and the repo owns **no floor instrument at all**. That
absence, not the score, is the finding.

**The retrain precondition is also smaller than #219 assumed.**
`build_dataset.py` at HEAD yields **1,224 train / 116 val over 164 problems**
(215 arms), **1,238 TypeScript against 102 Python**. Only **144 of the 499**
pool problems have ever produced a verified pass, and a training example
requires one. #219's "the corpus is now 499 problems" is wrong at the unit that
matters.

**ADR-0017** records the doctrine so it is derived from the repo and not from a
session: P1 floor beats ceiling and governs decomposition/pipeline/gates too;
P2 the 7B/14B were never the ceiling — the MoE 20B+ class is, and even it cannot
replace the API tier, so a ceiling gain is bounded on a track that structurally
cannot win; P3 the floor can move, so nothing hard-codes its tier. Three
rejected alternatives are recorded with it, the sharpest being *measure wherever
the instrument is sharpest* — retargeting to the 7B is better science on a worse
question, and is the same move that produced the drift in the first place.

**Three issues filed and wired as native GitHub dependencies**, not prose:
**#220** (this — re-read the plan against P1–P3), **#221** (how, and whether, to
train small models at all), **#222** (build whatever corpus #221 concludes for).
#221 is blocked by #220, #222 by #221, and all 63 other open issues are blocked
by all three — verified, not assumed.

## What this costs, stated plainly

ADR-0017 opens a gap it does not close: **nothing in the repo can currently
measure a small model getting better at work mcgyvr would actually hand it.**
Until a floor instrument exists, #221's question is unanswerable on evidence.

fine-tuning in Qwen2.5 bases.

## Graded afterwards, and it changed the shape of the answer

The first write-up of this lane said only that the 3B floors on the pool. Asked
where the collapse actually happens, I graded it across the difficulty rungs at
a common cap 2048: **`d1` 50.0%, `d2` 41.7%, `d3` 16.7%, pool 0%**. A **slope,
not a cliff** — `d3` at 16.7% still resolves gains and regressions, so the
usable band reaches further down than assumed. `d1`'s 50.0% reproduces the 50.6%
already in the records at a different cap and host, so the anchor is sound.

What that leaves unresolved is now the sharper question: **nothing occupies the
`d3`-to-pool range**, so the property that takes a 3B from 16.7% to zero is
unidentified, and every rung is `jsts`, so Python is unmapped. Filed as **#224**.

**#224 also fixes a dependency I had pointing the wrong way.** #113's first
scope bullet already demands "a task set … chosen so a small local worker
neither floors nor ceilings on it" — the floor instrument, specified in the
project's own words long before the pool was built. The doctrine was not
missing; it was written down and not followed. #113 cannot satisfy that bullet
without knowing where the model floors and ceilings, so the measurement is
**upstream** of #113 and #221, where I had originally wired it downstream.
Tree is now #220 → #224 → #221 → #222, with all 63 other issues blocked by all
four.

  50 3B pool rows is `stop_reason: complete` — but the justification was wrong
  and is now stated in terms of the stop reason.
