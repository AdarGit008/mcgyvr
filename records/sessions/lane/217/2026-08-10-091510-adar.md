# Session — lane/217 — 2026-08-10

## Did

Made the breadth rig's rows mean what they say, so the sweeps #225 and #224
will spend rig time on cannot lose a fifth of a run at exit 0.

**The defect.** `done_keys` counted any row as a recorded cell. A
`dispatch_error` row is the record of a draw nobody saw, so counting it meant a
resume skipped the cell forever — the 2026-08-08 srv2 outage cost 152 of 807
draws and re-running the identical command dispatched nothing.

**The mechanism, and why this one.** `done_keys` now excludes dispatch-error
rows, and `resume_state` moves them out of the append-only rows file before the
resume runs, keeping them verbatim in `dispatch-errors-invocation-<n>.jsonl` —
the name the manual recovery of that outage already left on disk. The rewrite
is recorded in `run.json` against the invocation that did it.

The two rejected options are recorded where the code is. Last-row-wins is the
worst: `pin.py._join_candidate` matches the **first** row for a cell, and a
dispatch-error row carries no `stop_reason`, so the reply corpus would die on
`KeyError` where every other provenance failure raises a diagnosable
`PinError`. That is now a test. A `--retry-dispatch-errors` flag was the
audit's leading answer and is rejected on the acceptance criterion's own
wording: it reproduces failure mode 1, *it requires noticing*. The
deliberateness the run-identity discipline asks for is bought by preserving,
announcing and recording — not by requiring foreknowledge.

**Incompleteness survives the scrolled-past line.** Every run now says whether
an observation reached every cell it meant to fill: a `completeness` block in
`run.json` naming the cells, a banner at the **head** of `summary.md` rather
than a count in its last line, and a non-zero exit. Derived from what manifests
already record (`draws` × the union of `invocations[].tasks`), so it answers
for every directory ever written — `--audit` asks the whole corpus at once.
**87 breadth-shaped directories judged, 0 holed**, which also says the fix
condemns no existing record. A bundle-shaped manifest answers *unjudgeable*
rather than *complete*; that distinction is a test.

**The contiguous-outage question, answered yes.** A run-level circuit breaker
at three consecutive tasks losing every draw to transport. It cannot fire on a
healthy backend — it needs every draw of three tasks to fail below the model —
and the observed outage would have cost 3 tasks instead of 51, about 4.7 of its
5 hours. The row-level rule is untouched: a failed draw is still a row, the
rows already written stay, and the resume fills them. A task the resume skipped
returns no rows and never advances the streak.

**Both drivers, one implementation.** `campaign.run_stage` reaches the resume,
the breaker and the stamp through `measure` rather than carrying a second copy;
a stopped stage raises, because the driver's next act would be to climb a
ladder on a rate computed over a hole.

**The bundle rig is left alone, deliberately.** It has the same `done_keys`
defect and cannot reach it — #240 retired both its arms and `record_run`
refuses every sweep. Its docstring now says so and says what porting would take,
including the part that is not a copy: a dispatch error on *its* retry leaves a
row that already carries a first reply and its capture.

`make check` green (1208 tests), `pin.py --check` matches all 12,331 replies —
no run's rows moved.
