## #354 — the arithmetic before the launch (`0a990d6a`)

ADR-0039's rule is `max_num_seqs × max_model_len × bytes_per_token` and it is
right. Three phase-0 cells refused on an **empty** card, and each declared figure
was exactly what the entry's own shape computes: Qwen3-4B is 36 × 8 × 128 × 2 ×
2 = 147,456 B/token and 65,536 tokens of it is exactly the declared
9,663,676,416 — 9.0 GiB on a card that holds 6.0.

**The rule settles what a model's bytes are. Whether they fit is a property of
the card, and that was unowned.** Every vLLM figure this project held came from
the 1.5B, 28 layers × 2 KV heads, four times narrower per layer than the models
that failed, so "bytes travel across cards" was read as though travelling and
fitting were the same property.

`declaration_fits` sits **after `release` and before the argument list**, and
both neighbours are the point. After the release, because the free memory a
declaration is checked against is the memory it will get, and the previous
engine has not let go until then — the refit's srv2 pair demonstrated it on the
wire, where the 14B's yield row correctly reads `card_used_mib: 11101,
card_idle: false` from the cell before it. Before the argument list, because not
spending the launch is the whole of its value.

Two sources, and the measured one wins: a host with a measured `_footprint_mib`
is judged on that, because a footprint the card produced needs no model of why
it fits. The refusal says which of the two it used.

**It names both ways out with a figure each and takes neither.** Which to give
up is the entry's decision; a launcher that chose silently is how a run comes to
measure a configuration nobody declared. The owner chose, and chose the one this
session would not have: width 8 held, `max_model_len` shortened.

## The constant the measurement refuted (`ec1f0c43`)

`NON_KV_OVERHEAD_MIB` shipped at **910**, assembled from ADR-0039's four terms —
470 MiB driver and CUDA context, 133 MiB peak activation, 51 MiB non-torch, one
256 MiB allocator block. **That sum double-counts.** `nvidia-smi`'s card figure,
which every footprint in this project is read from, already contains the
driver's reserve and the process's own context, so two of the four were charged
twice.

The refit measured the residue directly — `card_mib_after_load − weights −
declared_kv` — on three cells spanning 2.5 and 9.38 GiB of weights:

| cell | declaration | card | residue |
|---|---|---|---|
| srv1 / `Qwen3-4B-AWQ` | seqs 8 × len 2,048 | 5,222 MiB | 358 MiB |
| srv2 / `Qwen2.5-Coder-14B-AWQ` | seqs 8 × len 1,024 | 11,479 MiB | 337 MiB |
| srv2 / `Qwen3-4B-AWQ` | seqs 8 × len 7,168 | 11,101 MiB | 477 MiB |

337–477 MiB against the 910 the sum predicted: it over-stated every footprint by
433 to 573 MiB. And the residue **does not track model size** — the 14B, with
9.38 GiB of weights, holds the least of the three. It tracks the KV cache, which
is what the block padding sits on.

The constant is now **733 = 477 + 256**, the largest residue any cell has
produced plus the block a launch must still be able to take. Both halves are
readings, and the check re-derives both from the campaigns' own tables, so a
cell with a larger residue fails rather than quietly making the constant wrong.

The window every measured cell allows narrowed from **511–1,793 MiB** (phase 0's
seven) to **511–1,145** (all ten), the ceiling set by the 14B. No verdict changed
at either value, which is why ADR-0039 takes a correction to a *derivation*
rather than to a decision — appended and dated, never edited in place.

**Assembling a constant from parts is what let the double-count survive one
commit; measuring the whole residue is what caught it.** The lesson is not that
910 was careless — every term in it is a real measurement from ADR-0039's own
table. It is that a sum of correct parts is not a measurement of the whole, and
this file already knew that: it is the same distinction `_footprint_mib`'s note
draws when it says the arithmetic predicts the KV cache and only the card says
what the process took.

## The cell run at a margin on purpose

srv2's 14B was declared with 235 MiB of predicted slack — under one allocator
block — rather than backed off to a safer length. If it had refused, the
engine's own words would have said whether the constant needs a term that grows
with the model. **It loaded, with 808 MiB of real slack**, so the answer is that
the prediction was conservative rather than tight, and that reading is what set
the corrected constant. A cell chosen to be informative either way cost nothing
extra and settled the question the first constant could not.
