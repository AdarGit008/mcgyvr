**The owner's rule, and why it is not the same as equalising.** *No limits: the
hardware is the limit; if it breaks, we fix mcgyvr.* `MAX_LOADED_MODELS` and
`NUM_PARALLEL` are `0` — no cap — and `KEEP_ALIVE` is `-1`, so nothing is
evicted by a clock. But the values are **declared on both** rather than left to
the engine, because srv2's old values were not a known quantity: they were
whatever 0.32.5 chose, recorded nowhere, under a different engine version from
srv1's. `-1` resolves to `2562047h47m16.854775807s` — max int64 — on both.

**Verified, not assumed.** With `NUM_PARALLEL` declared, both rigs launched
`qwen2.5-coder:1.5b` at an identical `-c 4096 -np 1` and reported a
byte-identical `size_vram` of `1166236712` — across the version split and two
different cards. After the 11-version ollama jump the same reading reproduced
exactly. Cross-family co-residency was measured for the first time:
`llama3.2:3b` beside `qwen2.5-coder:1.5b`, **both 100% on GPU**, on srv1's 6 GB
card as well as srv2's 12 GB (3722 MiB of 6144; 3817 of 12288). The campaign
had never tested it — its only co-residency cell was qwen beside qwen.

**docker on srv1 was unrecorded and is now closed.** Docker 29.1.3, the docker
group, NVIDIA Container Toolkit 1.19.1, and the image pulled: inside the
container on srv1, torch 2.11.0+cu130 reports compute capability `(7, 5)` and
`get_arch_list()` contains `sm_75`. Turing is supported. That answers #329's
box 12 at no rig cost.

**A container does not pin the driver.** Inside srv2's container `nvidia-smi`
reports the host's `595.84`; srv1's host is `580.173.02`. The image pins CUDA
userspace, torch and kernels — the driver difference survives and stays
unmeasured. An earlier claim in this session that docker-on-both "erases the
host difference" was too strong and is corrected here.

**The two vLLM builds' declared surfaces are identical**, established at zero
rig cost by diffing `vllm serve --help=all`: **275 flags, 223 defaults, zero
differences**. That closes the declared half of the pip-vs-docker question. The
compiled half is what #329's rig arm would still measure.

## The design pass

The owner ruled: *"a campaign is just a bunch of small runs stacked; each run
has its own header and its own goal; each run verifies HW is clean and idle
before starting and leaves nothing behind."* Three independent designers ran on
it, then a verifier charged with attacking the brief they shared — the shared
blind spot being the brief itself.

**What it cost the plan.** The 60-cell matrix filed earlier in the session is
wrong in three ways, all verified:

1. **20 of the 60 cells cannot run.** `run.py:351` releases every other backend
   before each cell with no opt-out; `ollama.py:474` requires
   `card_idle_before_load is True`; `vllm.py` has no `residents()`. Run anyway,
   they produce a **valid solo vLLM curve under a co-residency label** with a
   false refusal reason — full cost, plausible data, wrong provenance.
2. **The order arm is half-real.** Both orders are expressible, but the harness
   always ramps the model loaded **second**, so no cell can measure a model
   that was loaded first.
3. **The cost was 3.5x over.** 1782 min was computed from a per-rig average
   inflated by models the ≤7B roster excludes. The runnable campaign is
   **~510 min — shorter than D7 itself.**

**What the verifier overturned in the owner's own ruling.** "Clean and idle" is
the wrong shape: **15 of 17 D7 cells carry `yielded.vllm.card_idle: False`**,
each of them the *previous* cell's footprint, cleared two seconds later by
`ollama.claim`'s own release. A literal idle precondition refuses 15 of 17 cells
over a card that is clean moments after — and it forbids the co-residency the
campaign exists to measure.

**The correction, ruled by the owner:** two rules, not one. What the header
declares must match or the cell refuses; **everything else is recorded, never
ignored.** Narrow on purpose, wide by instrument.

**And the tree keeps minting idle readings it never wires.** Three exist and
none is read by production code: `snapshot()["gpu_idle"]` (`contract.py:458`),
the `release()` return `vllm._start` discards (`vllm.py:588`), and
`gpu_compute_apps` (`contract.py:438`, its only appearance in the tree). A
fourth gate is not the fix.

## The records

**ADR-0038 — a machine has no role, and the question approves its own scope.**
Accepted. Amends ADR-0024, withdrawing clauses 1 and 2 (srv2 "the measurement
rig", srv1 "capacity", no cross-host rates). Their reason was ADR-0024's own
clause 3 — the serving build is run identity — and both rigs now run one build.
What still differs is card and driver, which is the **subject** of a cross-rig
question, not a confound in it. The roles had come to forbid #329, the question
they were written to protect.

D3-D5 are the owner's: the comparison check is **deliberately unaware** and is
not to be taught; an ignored difference is a record **on the contrast, never on
the cell**; a **one-armed cell is first class** and may become an arm of a
comparison nobody planned. Two alternatives are recorded as rejected — teaching
the check requires exactly the knowledge under test, and keeping the roles with
an exception list is the same mistake one level up.

**`docs/run-contract-2026-08-22.md`** states the contract: one header per
question, one process per cell, the two pre-state rules, the post-state rule
with its three failure owners, and five defects each with its `file:line`. Two
things in it deserve to survive: the honest cost is **9%, not 1%** (every cell
pays its own teardown), and **every cell is a cold start** because
`drop_page_cache` forces it — so these are calibration numbers and must never
be quoted as serving numbers.

**ADR-0037 is Accepted**, and it proved itself on the way. Its rule 3 refused
ADR-0038 the moment that record named four checks the suite did not hold —
`make check` went red — so the rule was enforced against the next record
written under it *before* it was ratified. `tests/test_run_contract.py` holds
