## Did

Delivered #51 — agent-supplied context as an accelerator — as
`src/mcgyvr/orchestrator/context.py`, plus the charging change it implies in
`read.py`.

The shape of the problem is that a hint arrives from something fallible: another
agent that may be stale, confused, or simply making it up. So the guarantees are
arithmetic and structural rather than stated intentions.

- `SuppliedContext(paths, contents)` is what a caller claims; `verify(index, …)`
  is what survives. An unknown path is dropped. Supplied text is compared to the
  indexed content line-for-line and dropped unless it matches exactly — the
  repository is the authority, and a stale copy cannot be fed back as fact. Every
  rejection becomes a `ContextFinding` (`UNKNOWN_PATH`, `STALE_CONTENT`,
  `UNCORROBORATED`), so contradiction is reported, never absorbed.
- `accelerate(resolution, verified)` re-ranks *only* the shortlist `resolve()`
  already produced. It cannot add a path or remove one, so the files a plan
  targets stay the deterministic pass's decision.
- The safety bound is the load-bearing part: `_HINT_BOOST` is *derived* from
  `resolve._DOMINANCE` (a fixed fraction of the gap) rather than written as a
  literal, so the inequality survives either constant being retuned. A `RESOLVED`
  leader beats its runner-up by at least `_DOMINANCE`; a boost strictly below
  `_DOMINANCE` therefore cannot overtake it. A wrong hint cannot re-point a
  resolved plan — arithmetically, not by convention.
- The saving lands in `explore()`: a region whose file verified as fresh is
  charged nothing, and `Exploration.saved` records what it would have cost.
  `TargetedRead` keeps its true `estimated_tokens` beside a `supplied` flag, so a
  free read stays auditable. Free regions are taken even past the budget cut —
  charging nothing, they cannot displace a paid region, and the deferral list
  remains a faithful account of where money ran out.

Exposed as `mcgyvr read --hint PATH` / `--holds PATH`, with rejected hints
printed. On this repo, `"resolve a target"` at a 3000-token budget goes from 5
regions to 7, with 1506 tokens saved and a hallucinated `--hint` reported and
dropped.

**One design correction worth recording.** The first cut claimed — in the
docstring and in a test — that a hint *settles* an ambiguity. It does not, and
should not. With the boost capped below dominance, two exactly-tied candidates
stay `AMBIGUOUS` however firmly one is named; the hint only reorders them. That
is the correct behaviour: a hint that could manufacture certainty from a dead tie
would be load-bearing, which is precisely what #51 forbids. What it *can* do is
finish a call the index had already nearly made. Docstrings and tests now say
that, and the distinction is covered by two separate tests.
