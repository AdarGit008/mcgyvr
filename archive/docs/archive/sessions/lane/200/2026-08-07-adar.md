# Session — lane/200 — 2026-08-07

## Did
#200 closed. A flake found during #167 turned out to be a missing instrument
rather than a timing tolerance. `peak` is per-source in both witnesses, so
nothing recorded whether two sources were ever in flight together — and the
mixed-batch test used a wall clock as a proxy, which cannot tell "the sources
never overlapped" from "the box was busy".

Capacity now tracks a global in-flight peak, exposed as `Concurrency`
(peak/total/saturated) from `concurrency()` — a separate method, because a
cross-source fact has nowhere honest to sit in a per-source record, and tracked
rather than derived because a maximum of sums is not the sum of maxima. Observer
grew the same counter independently so the new number keeps the cross-check the
per-source ones have (test_the_cross_source_peak_agrees_with_what_was_observed),
and a second test pins why it cannot be derived.

The real fix was not the counter. A sampled peak just moves the race — the first
attempt asserted peak_total == 5 and failed at 4, repeating the mistake being
fixed. Jobs now rendezvous on a barrier: each holds its slot until N parties hold
theirs, so a loaded box makes the test slower and never wrong, and a
series-draining executor fails on a deadlock-guard timeout nothing asserts on.
Also split safety from liveness in
test_no_source_ever_exceeds_its_declared_capacity: `<=` (no load can cause a
violation) stays on the sleeping jobs, `==` (the ceiling was reached) moved to
the barrier tests where it is deterministic.

Measured before: 2 failures in ~14 full-suite runs, 0 in 40 isolated runs under
load. After: 25/25 capacity-suite runs under eight-core load, 8/8 full-suite runs.
